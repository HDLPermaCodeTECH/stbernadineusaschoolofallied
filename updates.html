Google Search Console setup completed

The St. Bernadine website is now verified in Google Search Console. This service provides reports directly from Google about how people find the website and whether its pages can appear in search. Verification used the existing HTML verification file; that file remains in place to preserve ownership verification.

Current sitemap successfully processed

The current sitemap at https://stbernadineusa.com/sitemap.xml was submitted, and Google reported “Sitemap processed successfully” on November 18. Google discovered all 35 pages listed in this sitemap. Discovery means Google knows these addresses; it does not mean all 35 pages are already indexed or guaranteed to appear in search results.

Four priority pages submitted for a fresh crawl

Fresh indexing requests were accepted for the homepage, Placement, Home Care and CNA Program pages. The homepage is already indexed. The other three inspection records still showed older preferred addresses ending in .html and using www, based on crawls before the November 17 SEO update. Their preferred addresses were corrected in that release. Google accepted all four requests into its priority crawl queue and must now revisit and process the pages.

Search performance baseline recorded

The three-month Web search report, covering August 16 through November 15, 2026, showed 215 clicks, 2.25K impressions, a 9.6% average click-through rate and an average position of 7.5. Impressions count appearances in search results; clicks count visits from those results. Average position summarizes the positions recorded across searches and is not a fixed ranking for one keyword. These figures predate the November 17 release and serve as a baseline for later comparisons, rather than results attributed to the new update.

Historical indexing records reviewed

The indexing report last updated on November 13 showed 5 indexed and 87 not-indexed URLs across all known pages. That total includes older and duplicate addresses, redirects and previous errors. It is not a report of 87 failures among the 35 current sitemap pages. The older records need to be assessed as Google processes the updated website.

Historical server-error validation started

Google’s historical server-error record referred to /apply-job.html, last crawled on October 22. The current address redirects to /apply-job and returns HTTP 200 successfully. A validation request was started on November 18, and Google displayed “Validation started.” This validation remains pending; it has not yet been marked passed by Google.

What this update makes possible

The website now has a verified reporting channel for measuring search visibility and checking indexing problems. The submitted sitemap and individual crawl requests help Google discover and reassess the updated pages. Future comparisons can use clicks, impressions, search terms and page coverage to identify useful improvements. Search rankings, indexing decisions and processing times remain controlled by Google.

Completed work and next checkpoint

Completed today: ownership verification, successful submission of the 35-page sitemap, four accepted crawl requests, a recorded performance baseline and a validation request for the historical server error. Google’s recrawling and validation are still in progress. The next checkpoint is to review those results after processing. No website code was changed as part of today’s Search Console setup.

November 17, 2026Search visibilityClearer search information across the websitePage-specific titles, descriptions, preferred addresses, structured data and a refreshed sitemap across 35 public pages.ReleasedRead report

New SEO update released

The SEO update is now live across all 35 public pages of stbernadineusa.com. This release improves how the website describes its programs, services and resources to search engines, and makes the preferred page addresses consistent throughout the site.

Clearer search titles and descriptions

Every public page now has its own title and description, including pages that previously lacked a description. Program pages identify the relevant training and Jersey City or New Jersey location where appropriate. Placement, home care, inquiries and student resources use wording specific to their content. This helps visitors understand which page answers their needs when they see it in search results; search engines may choose their own displayed title or snippet.

Consistent page addresses and redirects

The preferred website address is https://stbernadineusa.com. Canonical tags—the page’s preferred address for search engines—now match the website’s clean URLs. Internal page links use those destinations directly. Old .html links, duplicate index addresses and supported case or trailing-slash variants permanently redirect to the correct page. Inquiry category parameters are preserved during these redirects.

Updated sitemap and crawler settings

The sitemap now lists all 35 public pages using their direct, preferred addresses, with the November 17 update date. It is published at stbernadineusa.com/sitemap.xml and referenced in robots.txt. Public pages and their assets remain available for crawling, while crawler instructions exclude the API and health-check paths. These instructions guide search engines; existing access controls continue to protect server files.

Accurate business and course information

Structured data is the machine-readable information that helps search engines understand a website. The organization name, Jersey City address, phone number and website identity are now consistent across the pages. Existing course information has been aligned with the correct page and provider. Registration fees are no longer presented in this markup as prices for an entire course.

Sharing previews and a broken link corrected

Page titles, descriptions, preferred addresses and the St. Bernadine logo are also aligned in the metadata used for social sharing previews. The broken application PDF download link now directs visitors to request a printable application through the Contact form. This avoids sending visitors to an unavailable download. The website’s layout, logo, hero images and bottom contact navigation were retained.

Verification and live release

All 76 automated checks passed, including the new SEO checks and the existing form, email and security regression checks. The production verification confirmed HTTP 200 responses and matching final source on all 35 public pages. The sitemap, robots.txt and Google verification file were checked, along with five representative permanent redirects, including the www address and a link containing an inquiry category. The new server-side routing file remains inaccessible to public requests. Form and email regression checks used local test fixtures rather than new department test deliveries.

What to expect next

The updated sitemap is published and discoverable through robots.txt; this release did not include a Google Search Console submission. Search engines need to crawl and process the updated pages before changes appear in results. Search positions and traffic improvements are not guaranteed by this release. The next useful measurements are indexing coverage, impressions and clicks in Google Search Console.

Checking requests before processing

Public submission endpoints were strengthened to reject unsupported request formats, oversized input and invalid nested field structures. Requests from untrusted origins are rejected before normal email processing. These checks reduce opportunities for malformed input to be treated as instructions.

Safer files and signatures

Permitted uploads are checked against their filename extensions and file contents. Photos and signatures have image-size limits. Temporary uploads receive random filenames, remain outside the public website folder and are cleaned up after processing. These controls reduce upload abuse; they are not antivirus scanning.

Limits on excessive processing

Concurrent upload and AI requests are limited alongside the existing request-rate controls. Browser security settings and affected dependencies were updated. These layers reduce abuse from manual requests, scripts and AI-assisted tools without promising that every possible attack can be stopped.

Maintenance and verification

The release included a fixed six-hour maintenance window for forms and inquiry pages with automatic expiry. The homepage and service information remained available. The release verification recorded 71 passing checks using simulated email and verification-service responses; no trial department emails were sent by those checks. This is a protection update, not evidence of a confirmed intrusion.

Verification on public forms

Cloudflare Turnstile was configured for public form submissions, including Contact, Placement Inquiry, Home Care Inquiry, Care Request, Job Application, Enrollment, Referral and newsletter signups. The server checks the verification result as well as the website and intended action; protection does not depend only on displaying a verification box.

Additional checks

Bot-trap fields, signed form tickets, duplicate-submission handling and request limits add protection against automated or repeated submissions. Recognizable unrelated commercial pitches are flagged for staff review. Generic words alone do not establish that a message is spam.

Direct email needs a different layer

Turnstile protects website submissions, not messages sent directly to an email address. Sales Review folders and filters were configured in the five department mailboxes to separate recognizable sales messages from ordinary inquiries. Matching messages remain reviewable instead of being automatically deleted.

Compatibility and presentation

The verification layout was made slimmer and centered. A service-worker compatibility issue affecting repeat visits was corrected. Floating controls were reduced and the homepage audio control was removed. The release report recorded 33 passing automated checks and 21 live form/viewport checks.

Practical limits

Legitimate service inquiries and relevant business partnerships should still be reviewed by the team. Filtering requires ongoing adjustment and cannot guarantee that every unsolicited offer will be stopped.
